Taiwan

Bubble Milk Tea / 珍珠奶茶 / 波霸

This is the iconic modern drink known today. It consists of brewed tea, milk, sweetener, and chewy tapioca pearls.

Origin and History

Bubble milk tea was invented in Taiwan during the 1980s. There were two rival teahouses at the time. The tea house Chun Shui Tang and Hanlin Tea Room both claimed to have independently invented bubble milk tea; the Taiwanese court ultimately decided that it is a shared cultural drink. After that, bubble milk tea quickly gained popularity, which rapidly expanded this local drink into a massive global trend.

Key Components

The tea base can be black tea, green tea, or oolong tea that is brewed and shaken until it turns bubbly like .

The milk can be plain milk, oat milk, or any other milk alternative.

The pearls are chewy balls made of sweet potato, starch, and brown sugar combined.

Japan

Tapioca Tea / タピオカティー

It became super trendy in Japan in 2019, leading to some Japanese teenagers creating a slang called "tapiru", which literally means "to go drink tapioca."

Origin and History

Bubble tea from Taiwan first got introduced to Japan in the late 1990s but remained very niche. Its popularity boomed in the late 2010s, driven by youth culture.

Key Components

The tea base is Japanese teas like roasted green tea or green tea.

The milk can be either Hokkaido milk, oat/soy milk, or cheese foam.

The pearls can be either brown sugar pearls, standard black pearls, or tapioca.

Thailand

Thai Milk Tea / ชาเย็นใส่ไข่มุก

It is a fusion of the Taiwanese bubble milk tea and the local Thai Iced Tea (Cha Yen).

Origin and History

Thailand integrated the Taiwanese boba culture with its own street food iced tea.

Key Components

The milk can be a combo of sweetened condensed milk layered with evaporated milk, or Japanese milk imports from Japan.

The pearls can be mochi pearls, soft brown sugar boba, or crystal pearls.

The tea base is an oxidized black tea, but stores would sometimes add additives like warm spices, orange food colorings, and dry tea leaves for aesthetic purposes.
